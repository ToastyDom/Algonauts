The Algonauts Project

The Algonauts Project, first launched in 2019, is on a mission to bring biological and machine intelligence researchers together on a common platform to exchange ideas and explore the intelligence frontier.

Inspired by astronauts’ exploration of space, “algonauts” explore biological and artificial intelligence with state-of-the-art algorithmic tools.

The 2027 Challenge

From passive perception to active interaction

A central goal of computational neuroscience is to predict the brain activity underlying perception, decision-making, and interaction with the environment.

Earlier Algonauts challenges used shared datasets and evaluation procedures to compare models predicting neural responses to images and videos during passive perception. The 2025 challenge broadened this perceptual focus to multimodal processing with movies combining visual and auditory input. Because cognition involves much more than perception, the 2027 challenge takes a leap from passive perception to active interaction through video gameplay. In this setting, perception and action form a closed loop. What players see and hear guides their decisions and actions, and those actions shape the sensory input they receive next.

Video games have an established role in both artificial intelligence and cognitive neuroscience. In artificial intelligence, they provide reproducible environments for comparing how agents learn and generalize. In cognitive neuroscience, researchers use games to study how the brain represents task states, learns rules, and responds to prediction errors. The 2027 challenge brings these research directions together.

In collaboration with the Courtois NeuroMod Project (CNeuroMod), we release gameplay data (video, audio, controller actions, and game event annotations) aligned with functional magnetic resonance imaging (fMRI) measurements. We ask teams to develop encoding models that predict brain activity during gameplay and generalize within and across games. Performance on out-of-distribution (OOD) games, kept hidden until the final phase, determines the final challenge ranking.
